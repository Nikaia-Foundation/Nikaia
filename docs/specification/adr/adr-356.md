# ADR-356: A `let` and a `for` Head Take a Nested Tuple of Names — No Variant

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** October 14, 2026
**Answers:** [#611](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/611)
(*a `for` head and a `let` take only one level of tuple*)
**Related:**
[ADR-291](adr-291.md) D1-D6 (the flat tuple this widens, its readers, `NK1136`, `_`);
[ADR-291](adr-291.md) D10 (`match` takes nested patterns);
Part I 2.1, 3.3; Part III C.1

## 1. Question

**How deep may a `let` and a `for` head take a tuple apart, and may either name a variant?**

ADR-291 D1 let a `let` bind one name or a flat tuple of names, and D5 refused a
tuple inside the tuple with a sentence. A `for` head took the same flat list.
`match` takes nested patterns (ADR-291 D10), so the same tuple
`(name, (line, col))` could be taken apart in an arm and not at a binding.

## 2. How others do it

| Language | Its answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| Rust | Any irrefutable pattern at `let` and `for`, nested; a refutable one needs `let … else` or `if let` |
| Python | Nested tuple targets at assignment and `for` |
| Swift, Kotlin | Swift nests tuple patterns at `let` and `for`; Kotlin's destructuring is one level |

## 3. Options, weighed

| Option | Why it won or lost |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A1 — both sites take a nested tuple of names, `_` and tuples, recursively** | Chosen: one pattern grammar for both binding sites, the one `match` already nests |
| A2 — only the `for` head nests | A `let` that stays flat would be the one binding site that takes a tuple apart differently from its neighbour |
| B — one level of nesting | An arbitrary depth: the second level is no different in kind from the first |
| A variant in the head | A variant can fail to match, and a binding has no branch to go to; that is `match`'s job |

ADR-291 D1's reason was what the programs of the day needed. That is not how
the language is decided: what it offers follows from what it is for and the
coherence of its forms, and a tuple taken apart in one place and not in another
is not coherent.

## 4. Decision

### D1 — A `let` and a `for` head take one irrefutable tuple pattern, nested as deep as it is written.

```nika
let (a, (b, c)) = (1, (2, 3))
for (name, (line, col)) in places { … }
for (key, (_, count)) in totals { … }
```

The pattern is a name, `_`, or a parenthesised list of two or more patterns.
`(p)` is `p`. Each name takes the part at its position; a part whose type is not
known is `?`, and a width is not compared where it is unknown (ADR-291 D3). `_`
binds nothing (ADR-291 D6). A written type on a destructuring `let` stays
`NK1136` (ADR-291 D4). Every reader that declined several names (ADR-291 D2)
declines a nested pattern the same way. *Changes ADR-291 D1 and D5.*

### D2 — A variant in a `let` or a `for` head is refused, `NK1259`, naming `match`.

`let Some(x) = o` and `for Ok(v) in results` can fail to match, and neither
site has another branch. The refusal says to take the value apart with a
`match`.
