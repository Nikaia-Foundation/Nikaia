# ADR-386: `drain()` Is on Every Map and Set, as on `Vec` and `HashMap`

**Status:** Accepted (#1130)
**Date:** November 1, 2026 (architect's decision under the owner's rule of
2026-10-31, `docs/supervisor.md`, *Language decisions*: an extension nobody
pays for who does not use it, in the form the language already has)
**Answers:** the C1b move (547e6462, `ledger_items_stored`) found that a
`collections::BTreeMap` has no `drain()` (`NK1171`), so its fold borrowed each
unit's map and cloned every set it took over
**Related:** [ADR-094](adr-094.md) D4 (a `for` lends; `drain` takes the
elements away), [ADR-collections-1](adr-collections-1.md) (the maps and sets
of `std::collections`), [ADR-384](adr-384.md) (the last `std` counterparts a
C1 move needed), Part III 17.1

## 1. Question

`Vec::drain` and `collections::HashMap::drain` are in the ledger
(`std.contracts`): a `for` over them binds owned elements and the container
goes with them. `BTreeMap`, `HashSet` and `BTreeSet` have no such entry, so a
program that wants to take a sorted map's values over must clone them. Is
`drain()` on them, and what does it mean?

## 2. Decision

### D1 - `drain()` takes every element out of a map or set

`m.drain()` on a `collections::BTreeMap[K, V]` is a `Seq[(K, V)] sync sized`,
on a `HashSet[T]` or a `BTreeSet[T]` a `Seq[T] sync sized`, with the
signature `HashMap::drain` has: the receiver is taken (`self`, not `ref`), so
what a `for` over it binds is the program's own, and a key, a value or an
element may be handed to something that keeps it. The two sorted containers
hand their elements out in key order and say `ends` as well, as their `iter`
does.

### D2 - Lowering

`HashSet` lowers to Rust's `drain()`. Rust's `BTreeMap` and `BTreeSet` have
no stable `drain`: they lower to `into_iter()` of the container, or to
`std::mem::take(&mut m).into_iter()` where the container is a place the
program still names after the loop (a field, a `let mut`); either is what D1
means.

## 3. Why (the principles)

* **Whoever uses it pays**: four ledger entries; a program that does not call
  them sees nothing.
* **Consistency**: ADR-094 D4 already says that taking elements away is
  `drain()`; it held for two containers of five only because nobody had needed
  the others.
* **Complexity into the compiler**: Rust spells the same thing three ways
  (`drain`, `into_iter`, `mem::take`); the program writes one word and the
  lowering chooses.
* **Other languages**: Rust has `HashMap::drain` and `HashSet::drain`, and
  `BTreeMap::extract_if` unstable; Swift's `removeAll` and Kotlin's
  `MutableMap.clear` after a copy do it in two steps. One word for every
  container is the simpler surface.

## 4. Not decided here

`drain(range)` on a `Vec` (no move has met it); `retain` beside it is
ADR-collections-1's.
