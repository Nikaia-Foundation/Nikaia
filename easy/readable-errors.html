Easy to understand

Readable errors

The problem
A compiler message that talks about lifetimes and borrowing sends you away from your own code.
An example
You change a value that you declared without mut.
What Nikaia checks
The compiler points at every place the explanation needs: ^ under the line with the mistake, - under the line that explains it. Then it says what to write instead. Errors point at your .nika line and do not talk about lifetimes or borrowing.
The limit
When no line of yours explains an error, the message says so. Where a check passes a problem on to the Rust compiler underneath, the error it raises is reported against your .nika line.
What the compiler says 
error[NK1139]: You're changing `count`, but it wasn't declared as mutable.
  --> app.nika:4:9
   |
 2 |     let count = 0
   |         ----- declared here without `mut`
   ...
 4 |         count += n
   |         ^^^^^ changed here
   |
   = help: Add `mut` where it's declared: `let mut count`.
